Network topology refers to the physical or logical arrangement of computers, devices, 
and communication links in a network. It defines how devices such as computers, switches, routers, and servers
are connected and how data flows between them.

Types of Network Topology

1. Bus Topology
All devices are connected to a single main cable called the backbone. 
Data sent by one device travels along the backbone and can be received by other devices.
Advantages: Simple, inexpensive, requires less cable.
Disadvantages: Failure of backbone can stop the entire network; performance decreases as devices increase.
.
2. Star Topology
All devices are connected to a central device, usually a switch or hub. Communication 
between devices passes through the central device.
Advantages: Easy to install and troubleshoot; failure of one cable affects only one device.
Disadvantages: Failure of the central device can bring down the entire network; requires more cable.

3. Ring Topology
Each device is connected to two neighbouring devices, forming a closed circular path. 
Data travels around the ring until it reaches the destination.
Advantages: Orderly data transmission; fewer chances of collision.
Disadvantages: Failure of one device or link can affect the network; troubleshooting is difficult.
